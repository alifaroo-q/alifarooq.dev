## The problem

I was the sole engineer on the backend of a workshop-management product: bookings, job cards, parts, invoicing, and the record each of those leaves behind. It grew to twenty-two modules, and the modules were the right shape. Each one owned its own tables and its own rules, and no module read another module's rows.

One booking crossed four of them. A job card was opened, parts were reserved against stock, an invoice was drafted, and a line was written to the audit trail. Four modules, four sets of writes, and one answer the customer had to get: all of it, or none of it.

A transaction is the answer to that, and a transaction is a handle. The plain way to give four modules the same handle is to pass it in. Every method that might take part in a write then grows a parameter, and every method that calls one of those grows it too. The parameter keeps spreading until the database sits in the signature of services that have no business knowing it exists.

## The decision

I drew the transaction boundary at the request, not inside any module.

A write that starts at one entry point runs to the end inside one transaction, and the modules under it never learn that it is there. A module still writes through its own repository and still owns its own tables. What changed is where the handle lives: it stopped being an argument and became something the request carries.

The diagram beside this is the shape of that. Twenty-two modules, four of them on one write, and a single boundary drawn around the four rather than through twenty-two sets of call signatures.

Then the next project needed the same thing, so I extracted it into `drizzle-tx`.

## The cost

Control flow you cannot see in a signature is control flow you can get wrong. Reading a service method no longer tells you whether it is running inside a transaction, and the answer is a call site away rather than in front of you. That is a real loss, and it is paid on every reading of the code rather than once when it is written.

I took it because the other price compounds. A handle threaded through twenty-two modules is an edit to all of them the day a twenty-third arrives, and it spends the module boundaries I had gone to the trouble of drawing.
